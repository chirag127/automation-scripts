# Astronomy in Ancient India

A brief overview of the history and achievements of Indian astronomy

By Chirag and Hardik

---

# Introduction

- Astronomy is one of the oldest sciences in human history
- India has a rich tradition of astronomical observations and calculations
- Indian astronomy influenced and was influenced by other cultures
- Indian astronomy has both religious and scientific aspects
- Indian astronomy spans from prehistoric times to modern days

# Main Body

## Pre-Vedic and Vedic Astronomy

- Some of the earliest forms of astronomy can be dated to the Indus Valley civilization or earlier
- Some cosmological concepts are present in the Vedas, such as the movement of heavenly bodies and the course of the year
- The Rig Veda describes time as a wheel with 12 parts and 360 spokes (days), with a remainder of 5, making reference to the solar calendar
- The Vedanga Jyotisha is the oldest known text on astronomy, dating to 1400–1200 BCE
- It describes rules for tracking the motions of the Sun and the Moon for ritual purposes

(Hardik)

## Classical Astronomy

- The classical era of Indian astronomy begins in the late Gupta era, in the 5th to 6th centuries CE
- Aryabhata is considered as one of the greatest astronomers of this period, whose work Aryabhatiya covers topics such as units of time, methods for determining planetary positions, causes of day and night, and other cosmological concepts
- He also introduced the concepts of elliptical orbits, heliocentrism, trigonometry, and zero
- Other astronomers who elaborated on Aryabhata's work include Brahmagupta, Varahamihira, and Lalla

(Chirag)

## Medieval Astronomy

- Indian astronomy was influenced by Greek, Persian, Islamic, and Chinese astronomy during this period
- Many Indian works on astronomy were translated into Arabic and Persian, and vice versa
- Many astronomical instruments were invented or improved by Indian astronomers, such as gnomon, armillary sphere, astrolabe, sundial, etc.
- Jai Singh II of Amber constructed five astronomical observatories called Yantra Mandirs in the early 18th century, which have several huge instruments based on Hindu and Islamic astronomy
- The Kerala school of astronomy and mathematics made significant contributions to calculus, infinite series, trigonometry, etc.

(Hardik)

## Modern Astronomy

- Indian astronomy was gradually displaced by European astronomy during the colonial period
- However, there were attempts at harmonising the Hindu and Islamic traditions of astronomy with modern science
- Some scholars have suggested that some results of the Kerala school may have been transmitted to Europe through trade routes and Jesuit missionaries
- India has made significant advances in space science and technology since independence
- The Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) has launched several satellites, probes, rockets, etc. for various purposes

(Chirag)
